## Good slide design

### Accessibility and Universal Design

- Make materials available online
- Large font size
- Don't use _only_ color to distinguish things

--

## Good slide design

### Don't get too cute

When in doubt, keep it simple. Overdone slides are often distracting.

- Simple fonts
- Simple colors
- Simple layouts

--

## Good slide design

### Content: Less is more

- Not too much text
- One or two important images

--

## Good slide design

### Layout

- Leave a margin in case projector is misaligned
- If possible, add a footer with slide numbers.

--

## Good slide design

### Text content

- **Size**: Leave the font size large—24 is an absolute minimum (prob still too small)
- **Color**: Ensure sufficient contrast between background and text.
  - Often an issue when using images instead of a solid-color background.

--

## Good slide design

### Slide backgrounds

- Use solid colors instead of an image (and certainly not animations)
- White/black are great; colors can work if your presentation has that vibe
- Instead of pure white/black, consider a softer option (off-white, very dark gray)

## Good slide design

### Slide backgrounds

**Three ways to combat low-contrast readability issues when using images as backgrounds**

1. Put a solid background behind the text, above the background image (warning: aesthetically meh)
2. Add a black or white tint/overlay to the background image
3. Only use a background image on title frames with extra-large text

--

## Good slide design

### Animation: good uses

- Highlight connections between slides
- Animate an analysis to accompany the music

--

## Good slide design

### Animation: bad uses

- looping GIFs
- silly slide transitions
- bullet-by-bullet reveals (too much clicking!)

Note: animations will not come through in a PDF version of your slides.
