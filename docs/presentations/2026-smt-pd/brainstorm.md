## Notes

- not really about "tech"
  - presenting is a lot like teaching—manage your time, draw your audience in
  - scripts
    - be super clear about your thesis statement and "signpost" your whole talk
    - remember to write in your speaking/presenting voice, not your writing voice. it's ok to be a little more casual—it's more engaging and authentic.
    - if printing
      - print in a large font
      - put your page turns in purposeful places
      - mark slide transitions
      - put it in a binder or otherwise make it so you can't lose your order
    - be respectful of the time limit
    - extemporizing is nice if you are confident in your ability to manage time.

- presenter view
  - use it, it has a timer
- keep your presentation online so you can use anyone's device to present
- slide design
  - PPT is not your only option: Keynote is easier for Mac folks. Some people are alleging that Google uses your documents, including Slides, ot train their AI, so maybe not that.
  - avoid funky backgrounds at least for content-ful slides
  - not too much text—avoid full sentences (AI doesn't get this)
- YouTube videos: DON'T. you will be cursed.
  - Tools for downloading: use your computer's built-in screen recording tool + something like Blackhole
- audio
  - get some audio in early as an attention-grabber
  - always prompt your audience what they should be listening for
  - use Audacity to trim your clips
  - use fade in/out to make the endings a little nicer (I think Keynote does this by default)
- handouts
- QR codes
