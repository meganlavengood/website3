5/4b: "Screening date" doesn't mean, "we don't look at applications until this date," it means, "after this date, we can move forward." This job was posted 18 days ago, it's the end of the academic year, and they probably need to move fast, especially if they want folks to be teaching actual classes on their in-person interview. Chances are extremely good that they've been screening applications as they came in (which is very common, especially in fast searches).
5/9: with 6 days between the request for a Zoom interview and Campus interviews, there's no way this isn't an internal hire.
5/10: This is a typical timeline for this time of year. Committee members conduct their individual reviews as applications roll in. The full committee meets for an hour on the "review begins" day, and later that day, 6-8 Zoom invites can go out. The Zooms can occur 3-5 days after the invites and take the committee 2-3 hours to complete, followed by an hour to name and email 3 finalists. During the term, searches take time in order to accommodate faculty and candidate schedules. Administrative hurdles can also slow things down. But after concerts/classes are finished and as faculty contract periods are about to end, the events of a search can occur with normal rigor but in quick succession. I hope this helps demystify the process. Searches can be a black box, and I wish I had known how they worked before I first went on the market.
5/10b: Thank you for clarifying. I think it’s important to remember that a fast timeline does not necessarily mean a search is illegitimate, especially this late in the academic year. Departments may simply need to move efficiently in order to have someone in place for fall teaching. Searches can absolutely still be rigorous even when they move quickly.
5/15: No affiliation with this search or institution, but to echo 5/10, this is very normal. Keep in mind the entire committee isn't looking at every single application. The individual members will have looked at them all and given them numerical rankings based on the minimal and preferred qualifications. Then, they will discuss their rankings and talk more about individual applications if there is a big discrepancy in the numerical score. Then, they'll look at the highest ranked applications and invite them for Zoom interviews. All of this can be done in 1-2 days. After Zoom interviews, it takes maybe an hour or two to decide on finalists. If everyone involved is motivated and there isn't a ton of bureaucracy, this can all easily be done in less than 6 days. I was hired over a summer and there were maybe 1-2 days between the zoom invite and the zoom interview and then my campus invite. And maybe 2 days after my campus interview I got the offer. And if they have a strong internal candidate who is already doing the job, they should hire them! But more often than not, the internal candidate doesn't get the permanent job. The internal candidate isn't your enemy. The process is just opaque, and honestly, before I was on a search committee myself, I thought all the same things. But this is all completely normal.
